## Authentication pathways

Every way to sign in or call the API, with its strength and protections (ASVS V6.1.1, V6.1.3, V6.3.4). There are no other pathways; a new one needs a row here and an ADR. Per-IP limits are multiplied only in the load-test environment (`IP_THROTTLE_MULTIPLIER`). The WAF adds a per-IP rate rule on every ALB.

Per-workspace budgets (ADR-0172) sit next to the per-IP and per-account limits, against credential stuffing spread over many addresses: failed password sign-ins (the larger of 100 and 5% of the workspace's active people per 15 minutes), failed authenticator codes (50 or 2%) and emailed links (100 or 2%). Failures only count towards the first two, so a busy workspace's successful sign-ins never use them. When a budget is spent, password sign-in (or code entry) in that workspace waits for the window: a malicious lockout of one workspace's password sign-ins is possible for 15 minutes, while SSO, passkeys and existing sessions keep working, and the refusal is a high-severity `auth.throttled` event. Every failure, lockout and refusal is an `auth.*` security event (see "Security logging").

New workspaces require two-step verification for everyone; workspaces that existed before migration 0024 keep "admins only" until an admin turns it on (ADR-0170).

## Passwords

- 12 to 128 characters of any kind; no composition rules; used exactly as typed; no expiry (ASVS V6.2.1 to V6.2.10). Fields are `type=password` and allow paste and password managers.
- Refused at sign-up and on change: the 10,000 most common passwords of that length from a public breach compilation (bundled, offline), and passwords containing a context-specific word (ASVS V6.1.2, V6.2.4, V6.2.11, V6.2.12; ADR-0132).
- Context-specific words: the product name (`thapsus`, `thapsushr`), every word of four or more letters or digits in the workspace name and slug (and the whole slug), and in the person's own name and email address (local part and domain labels other than the top-level one). Generic company suffixes (`limited`, `company`, `group`, `holdings`) and mail-provider labels (`gmail`, `outlook`, `hotmail`, `yahoo`, `icloud`, `example`, `mail`) do not count.
- Changing a password needs the current one; setting a first password (people invited by link or SSO) needs a recent sign-in or re-authentication. Every other session ends.
- **Forgotten passwords (ADR-0171).** Anyone can ask for a reset link from the sign-in page; the answer and its timing are the same whether or not the account exists. The link works once for 30 minutes, applies the policy above, ends every session, clears a lockout, is audited and is followed by an email to the person. It never signs anyone in and never removes a second factor (ASVS V6.4.3). Someone who signed in with an emailed link may set a new password without the current one within the 10-minute re-authentication window (the reasoning is in ADR-0171).
- **Who can reset someone else's sign-in.** Only a person whose company-wide grants include `settings.manage` and cover every permission the target holds, after re-authenticating, and never for themselves (`assertAccountAuthority`). They can email a reset link (24 hours; they never see or set the password), reset a lost second factor after confirming identity out of band, end every session, and unlink a replaced SSO account. Thapsus staff cannot.
- Customers who want stronger rules enforce SSO with their own IdP policy.

## Sessions

- **Lifetimes (ASVS V7.1.1, V7.3.1, V7.3.2).** Idle timeout 30 minutes and absolute lifetime 12 hours by default, which matches NIST SP 800-63B for AAL2 (re-authenticate after 30 minutes of inactivity and at least every 12 hours). Tenants may set 5–720 idle minutes and 1–720 absolute hours. Longer values are the customer's documented choice, for example for shared shop-floor devices, which use kiosks instead of personal sessions where possible. **Admin sessions** last at most 15 idle minutes and 8 hours whatever the tenant sets, and re-authenticate when their network (autonomous system) changes where the cell has that signal (`CLIENT_ASN_HEADER`; none today, ADR-0170). **Staff sessions**: 15 idle minutes, 4 hours absolute, and a key assertion within 5 minutes before destructive actions (ADR-0173).
- **Concurrency (V7.1.2).** A person may hold any number of sessions (phone, laptop, PWA). Each is listed on their Security page, where they can end any or all of the others; admins with company-wide `settings.manage` can end one person's sessions or everyone's.
- **Re-authentication (V7.5.1).** Replacing or adding a second factor when one exists, setting a first password, ending your own other sessions, signing everyone out and resetting someone's factors need a sign-in or `POST /v1/auth/reauth` within 10 minutes. Email addresses cannot be changed by the person (they come from SCIM or the employer).
- **Federation (V7.1.3, V7.6.1).** An SSO sign-in creates an ordinary Thapsus-HR session with the tenant's lifetimes; it does not extend or end the IdP session, and ending the IdP session does not end ours (no back-channel logout yet). Deprovisioning through SCIM ends every session at once. Sessions start only from a user action (no silent or IdP-initiated sign-in, V7.6.2).
- **Termination (V7.4.1 to V7.4.4).** Logout revokes the session server-side, clears the cookie and sends `Clear-Site-Data: "cache"`; the web client first sends any queued clock punches and then reloads the sign-in page so nothing stays in memory.

## File handling

What each upload accepts and how files are made safe (ASVS V5.1.1). Files are stored only in the home-region bucket under the tenant prefix with generated keys, are never executed, and are downloaded through 5-minute presigned URLs (or the authenticated API) as attachments with an RFC 6266 name. A file that fails a check is refused (or, for direct uploads, deleted and the document marked rejected). Known-malware scanning is not in place yet (HUMAN_TODO 1.7). Documents HR attaches to a person already pass through the scan hook (`apps/api/src/lib/upload-scan.ts`) before they are stored. It records `not_scanned` until a scanner is configured, and an `infected` verdict stores nothing (ADR-0165).

## Browser storage

The browser keeps only (ASVS V14.3.3): the HttpOnly session cookie; theme and locale preference cookies; on shared kiosks, the kiosk device token in `localStorage` (a device credential, revocable, ADR-0038); and, in the PWA, clock punches recorded while offline in `localStorage` (time, kind and, only where geofencing applies, coordinates to 4 dp) until they are sent. Signing out sends queued punches first; punches that cannot be sent (offline) stay for that person's next sign-in on the device (ADR-0036). The service worker caches only static assets and the offline page, never API responses or authenticated HTML.

## Cryptographic inventory and key management

## Dependency and secret scanning

Brief §8 and Phase 12.4. Every scan below is a step of `pnpm verify`, which CI runs on every pull request and every push to `main` (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`). A finding fails the run, so nothing merges with a known high or critical advisory or a committed credential.

| Scan             | What it checks                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Where                                             | Proof                              |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Dependency audit | `pnpm audit --audit-level high` over the whole lockfile, development tools included; fails on any high or critical advisory                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | verify `security` step                            | the step itself                    |
| Secret scan      | Every tracked and untracked, non-ignored file (git-ignored `.env` files are skipped) for AWS key ids and secret keys, private key blocks, GitHub, Stripe live, Slack and Google API tokens, and quoted password or secret assignments of 16+ characters. Fictional passwords are allowed only in test files and the local Docker defaults, never real credential formats | verify `security` step, `scripts/secret-scan.mjs` | `scripts/test/secret-scan.test.ts` |
| SBOM             | CycloneDX SBOM of the production dependencies; every component from registry.npmjs.org with an integrity hash; kept as the `sbom-cyclonedx` CI artifact                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | verify `security` step, `scripts/sbom.mjs`        | `scripts/test/sbom.test.ts`        |
| Pinning          | Exact versions from `pnpm-lock.yaml` (`--frozen-lockfile` in CI and images)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | `pnpm install` in CI                              | CI install step                    |

**When a scan fails.**

1. **Dependency audit:** upgrade, patch (`pnpm.overrides`) or remove the package on the branch that found it, within the timeframes under "Dependency remediation". If no fixed version exists yet and the vulnerable code is unreachable, record the advisory, why it is unreachable and a review date under "Accepted risks", and add it to pnpm's audit ignore list (`auditConfig`) with the same note. Never lower the audit level.
2. **Secret scan:** treat a hit as a leaked credential until proven fictional. Do not push. If it was already pushed, follow `docs/runbooks/incident-and-breach-response.md`: revoke and rotate the credential at its issuer (Secrets Manager for ours), then remove it from the branch. Rewriting published history does not un-leak a secret, so rotation always comes first. A fictional test value is fixed by shortening it or building it at run time, not by widening the allow-list. Allow-list entries need an exact path and rule and a code review.
3. **SBOM:** a component from another registry or without an integrity hash is removed or replaced.

GitHub's own secret scanning with push protection and Dependabot alerts are a second line on the repository; enabling them is an owner setting (HUMAN_TODO 5.3).

## Dependency remediation

ASVS V15.1.1. Every pull request and `main` run `pnpm audit --audit-level high` and the SBOM check. Known vulnerabilities in a dependency are fixed (upgrade, patch or remove) within: **critical** 7 days, **high** 30 days, **moderate** 90 days, **low** at the next routine update. If no fix exists, record the exposure and mitigation in "Accepted risks" within the same time. Dependencies are otherwise reviewed and updated at least monthly, and a package with no release for two years is replaced when a maintained alternative exists.

### Trust boundaries

1. **Internet → cell** (WAF → ALB → web / api): browsers, PWA, kiosks, API clients, IdPs (SAML/OIDC), SCIM clients, Stripe webhooks.

5. **Back-office staff → cell**: separate staff accounts with MFA; tenant personal data only through customer-granted support access.
6. **CI/CD → AWS**: GitHub Actions through OIDC deploy roles, per region.

## Accepted risks and follow-ups

- **Closed 2026-10-24 (security hardening P0):** automation webhooks and the `http` integration now go through `postOutbound` (V1.3.6); SSO binds people to (connection, issuer, subject) (V6.8.1, V10.5.2); IdP MFA counts only when the IdP asserts it and trust is off by default (V6.8.4); SAML is bound to the starting browser; staff sign in with passkeys only, so no generated staff password exists (V6.4.1); new workspaces require two-step verification for everyone (V6.3.3). See ADR-0170 to ADR-0173.
- **A workspace's password sign-ins can be throttled for 15 minutes by someone who spends its failure budget** (plan 2.1.6, ADR-0172). SSO, passkeys and existing sessions keep working; the event is high severity. Follow-up: WAF challenge or CAPTCHA after anomalies (plan 2.1.6, P1 cost). Owner: Security.
- **Admin re-authentication on a network change has no signal yet** (plan 2.1.5): neither the ALB nor Cloud Run adds an ASN, and an ASN database would be a new data source. The code honours `CLIENT_ASN_HEADER` once a trusted proxy (for example CloudFront) supplies it (ADR-0170). Owner: Platform.
- **Staff keys are not attested**, so a platform passkey (Face ID, Windows Hello) is accepted as well as a FIDO2 hardware key (plan 2.2.1 asks for hardware keys). Follow-up: require attestation from an allow-list of key models once the staff hold them (HUMAN_TODO 5.1). Owner: Security.
- **No known-malware scanning of uploads** (ASVS V5.4.3). Recurring cost, so it waits for approval: HUMAN_TODO 1.7. Owner: Platform.
- **Traffic between the ALB (and Service Connect) and the containers is plain HTTP inside the VPC** (ASVS V12.3.3). It never leaves private subnets and security groups allow only the ALB and the web service. Follow-up: TLS on the target groups with certificates from a private CA, once the domain exists. Owner: Platform.
- **Database and Valkey use static, generated passwords from Secrets Manager** rather than IAM authentication or short-lived tokens (ASVS V13.2.1). Follow-up: RDS Proxy IAM authentication and Secrets Manager rotation. Owner: Platform.
- **SSO binding is per connection.** The first sign-in through a connection links the person found by email; a customer admin who controls a newly added connection could therefore link an existing person on it once. Connections are created only by the customer's admins, JIT needs a verified domain, and every new binding is an `auth.sso_identity_bound` event. Owner: Security.
- **A temporarily locked account answers "locked"** on password sign-in, so someone who triggers a lockout learns that the account exists (account enumeration; ASVS V6.3.8 is level 3). Unknown accounts take the same time as wrong passwords, and the emailed-link and reset requests answer identically (plan 2.1.7). Follow-up: answer a lockout like a wrong password and tell the person by email. Owner: Security.
- **Emailed sign-in links are not bound to the browser that asked for them** (ASVS V6.6.2), so they work on another device by design. They are single-use, 256-bit and expire after 10 minutes, and anyone with a second factor must still use it. Owner: Product.
- **Credentials in URLs** (ASVS V14.2.1): calendar feed tokens (read-only, revocable, ADR-0033) because calendar clients cannot send headers, and emailed sign-in, invitation and reset links (each scoped to its purpose, short-lived, removed from the address bar and history at once; ADR-0171). The staff enrolment token is in the URL fragment, which never reaches a server. Session tokens and API keys never appear in URLs. Owner: Security.
- **Offline clock punches are kept in browser storage until sent** (ASVS V14.3.3; see "Browser storage"). Owner: Product.
- **RSA-2048 keys from outside parties** (ASVS V11.2.3): passkeys may use RS256 (Windows Hello) and customer IdPs may sign with RSA-2048 (112-bit security). Our own keys and algorithms give at least 128 bits. Owner: Security.
- **The back office is served from the app's host name** under `/backoffice` (ASVS V3.5.4). Staff use separate accounts, a separate SameSite=Strict cookie and separate API routes. Follow-up: a dedicated `backoffice.<cell>.<domain>` host once the domain exists (HUMAN_TODO 2.1, 2.2). Owner: Platform.
- **A sole owner who loses every second factor cannot be recovered in the product** (ASVS V6.4.4 covers everyone else through an admin reset). Customers are advised to keep two owners. Follow-up: a staff-assisted, identity-proofed recovery. Owner: Security.
- **Two-step verification for everyone can be turned off** by a workspace admin (after re-authenticating), and workspaces that existed before migration 0024 keep "admins only" until an admin turns it on (ADR-0170). Many frontline employees have no work phone, so a password-only sign-in stays possible for non-admin roles where the customer chooses it; their access is limited to their own records and team. Mitigations: breached-password and context checks, throttles and lockout, idle and absolute timeouts, least-privilege roles, audit. Owner: Product.
- **`uuid` 8 below 11.1.1 through `exceljs`** (GHSA-w5hq-g745-h8pq, moderate): the missing bounds check only applies when a caller passes its own buffer to v3, v5 or v6. `exceljs` 4.4 only calls `v4()` without a buffer, so it is unreachable. Review by 2027-01-21 (90 days) or when `exceljs` moves to a fixed `uuid`. Owner: Platform.
- **Independent penetration test** before production: HUMAN_TODO 5.2. Owner: Security.
- **CDK bootstrap execution policy.** By default `cdk bootstrap` grants the CloudFormation execution role AdministratorAccess. HUMAN_TODO 1.5 asks for a scoped policy at bootstrap time. The deploy roles themselves can only assume the bootstrap roles in their own region.
- **Logger scrubbing is allow-list based for class instances.** Request and reply objects are reduced by serializers, and plain objects are scrubbed recursively. Code review rule: never log ORM or row objects directly.
